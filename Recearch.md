The progressive volume loss $V_{wear}$ and localized wear depth $w(x, y, t)$ across the die tool surfaces (e.g., radius $R_{die}$) are modeled as:$$w(x, y, t) = \int_{0}^{t} K_{wear} \cdot P_{local}(x, y, \tau)^\gamma \cdot v_{slide}(x, y, \tau) \, d\tau$$Where $K_{wear}$ represents the empirical wear coefficient ($\text{m}^2/\text{N}$ or $\text{mm}^3/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m})$), $P_{local}$ is the localized normal contact pressure ($\text{MPa}$), $v_{slide}$ is the relative sliding velocity ($\text{m/s}$), and $\gamma$ is the empirical non-linear contact pressure exponent capturing plastic asperity deformation under extreme die loading.During continuous stamping operations, the applied normal load generates intense localized contact pressures along the die entry radius. The resulting material detachment transitions through distinct phases. Initially, the intact coating operates under a stable boundary film where friction remains constant. As material wear progresses past a critical threshold, the protective thin film fails, triggering micro-spalling and galling. This leads to direct tool-substrate contact, causing a rapid jump in localized contact pressure and an exponential increase in friction.Friction Evolution ModelThe local friction coefficient $\mu_{local}$ evolves dynamically as a function of the local wear depth ratio $w_{ratio} = w(x,y,t) / t_{coating}$:$$\mu_{local}(w_{ratio}) = \begin{cases}  \mu_0 + \alpha_f \cdot \left(\frac{w(x,y,t)}{t_{coating}}\right)^\beta & \text{for } \frac{w(x,y,t)}{t_{coating}} < W_{crit} \\ \mu_{substrate} + (\mu_{max} - \mu_{substrate}) \left[1 - \exp\left(-\xi \left(\frac{w}{t_{coating}} - W_{crit}\right)\right)\right] & \text{for } \frac{w(x,y,t)}{t_{coating}} \ge W_{crit} \end{cases}$$Where $\mu_0$ is the un-worn baseline friction coefficient under boundary lubrication ($\mu_0 \approx 0.08 - 0.18$), $\alpha_f$ is the friction acceleration coefficient, $\beta$ is a shape parameter ($\approx 1.0 - 1.5$), $t_{coating}$ is the nominal initial coating thickness ($2.0 - 4.0\ \mu\text{m}$), $W_{crit}$ is the critical wear ratio threshold ($0.70 - 0.85$) signaling localized coating removal, and $\mu_{substrate}$ reflects direct tool steel to 316L stainless steel contact ($\approx 0.65 - 0.75$), driving catastrophic adhesive galling.Boundary Conditions & Physical Validity LimitsNormal Contact Pressure ($P_{local}$): $50\text{ MPa} \le P_{local} \le 1200\text{ MPa}$ (concentrated along $R_{die}$).Sliding Speed ($v_{slide}$): $0.5\text{ mm/s} \le v_{slide} \le 50\text{ mm/s}$.Temperature Limits: $20^\circ\text{C} \le T \le 120^\circ\text{C}$ (accounting for frictional heating at high stroke rates).Validity Domain: Applies strictly to boundary-lubricated sheet metal forming using synthetic ester or water-soluble oils.Derivation & Physical RationaleDuring high-speed stamping of 316L, severe plastic deformation generates localized shear stresses at die fillets ($R_{die}$). Uncoated or monolayer PVD coatings (e.g., TiN, CrN) experience sub-surface micro-yielding due to the lower hardness modulus of the tool steel substrate. Duplex surface treatments (Plasma Nitriding + PVD TiAlN/CrN) introduce a functional gradient layer ($50 - 75\ \mu\text{m}$ nitrided depth, surface hardness $>900\text{ HV}$) that prevents "ice-crust" cracking under high local load. In the digital twin, as $w(x,y,t)$ approaches $W_{crit}$, the sharp jump in $\mu_{local}$ alters the sheet draw-in tension. Ductile fracture initiation is modeled via the Normalized Cockcroft-Latham (NCL) criterion:$$C_{crit, NCL} = \int_{0}^{\bar{\varepsilon}_f} \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_{eq}} \, d\bar{\varepsilon}_p \le C_{th}$$Where $\sigma_1$ is the maximum principal tensile stress ($\text{MPa}$), $\sigma_{eq}$ is the Von Mises equivalent stress ($\text{MPa}$), $\bar{\varepsilon}_p$ is the cumulative equivalent plastic strain, and $\bar{\varepsilon}_f$ is the equivalent plastic strain at failure. The flow stress $\sigma_{eq}$ during plastic deformation obeys the Hollomon power-law constitutive model:$$\sigma_{eq} = K_{strength} \cdot (\bar{\varepsilon}_p + \varepsilon_0)^{n_{hardening}}$$Where $K_{strength}$ is the strength coefficient ($\text{MPa}$), $n_{hardening}$ is the strain-hardening exponent, and $\varepsilon_0$ is the yield offset strain.Micro-stamping tooling geometry, characterized by die radius $R_{die}$ and draw angle $\theta_{die}$, directly establishes the strain path during sheet deformation. Under the Hollomon strain-hardening law, the material undergoes progressive strain hardening, concentrating tensile stress states ($\sigma_1 / \sigma_{eq}$) along the channel shoulders. The Cockcroft-Latham damage integral accumulates plastic strain history throughout the forming stroke. When the integrated damage value remains below $C_{crit,NCL} \approx 0.35$, the sheet exhibits uniform draw-in and thinning. If local strain exceeds $C_{crit,NCL}$, micro-cracking initiates, leading to foil rupture.Die Tooling & Geometry CouplingThe local stress concentration and damage accumulation rate $dC_{NCL}/dt$ depend directly on die design parameters:$$\frac{dC_{NCL}}{dt} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1(R_{die}, \theta_{die}, W/H)}{\sigma_{eq}} \right) \cdot \dot{\bar{\varepsilon}}_p$$Where $R_{die}$ is the die shoulder fillet radius ($0.10 - 0.25\text{ mm}$), $\theta_{die}$ is the draft angle ($7^\circ - 15^\circ$), and $W/H$ is the channel width-to-depth aspect ratio.Boundary Conditions & Physical Validity LimitsFoil Thickness ($t_{foil}$): $50\ \mu\text{m} \le t_{foil} \le 100\ \mu\text{m}$.Strain Rate ($\dot{\bar{\varepsilon}}_p$): $10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1} \le \dot{\bar{\varepsilon}}_p \le 10^{2}\text{ s}^{-1}$.Grain Size Ratio Constraint: $t_{foil} / d_{grain} \in [2.5, 8.0]$.Physical Limit: When $t_{foil} / d_{grain} < 3$, deformation heterogeneity across individual surface grains demands crystal plasticity finite element analysis (CPFE); the continuum NCL model is valid for $t_{foil} / d_{grain} \ge 3$.Derivation & Physical RationaleIn micro-forming, surface grains face fewer spatial constraints than interior grains, reducing the effective dislocation pile-up density and leading to lower macroscopic flow stresses. The mechanical constitutive response is modeled using the non-linear tangent modulus formulation proposed by Kleemann et al. and Bahrami et al.:$$E_{tangent}(P) = \frac{dP}{d\varepsilon} = E_0 \left(1 + \frac{K_s}{E_0} P\right)^2$$Integrating this tangent modulus with respect to compressive pressure $P$ yields the explicit non-linear strain $\varepsilon(P)$ and compressed thickness $t_{comp}(P)$:$$\varepsilon(P) = \frac{P}{E_0 + K_s P} \implies t_{comp}(P) = t_0 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{P}{E_0 + K_s P}\right)$$Where $E_0$ is the zero-pressure initial elastic modulus ($\text{MPa}$), $K_s$ is the non-linear stiffening factor, $t_0$ is the uncompressed GDL thickness ($\mu\text{m}$), and $P$ is the local compaction pressure ($\text{MPa}$).Applying stack assembly clamping load generates a non-linear strain response within the porous GDL matrix. As fibers compress, the overall bulk porosity decreases, shifting structural dimensions and inducing two coupled physical phenomena. Beneath the flow channels, unconstrained fiber bending leads to channel intrusion, which increases reactant gas transport resistance. Concurrently, beneath the land/rib areas, high compaction forces flatten surface micro-asperities. Applying higher clamping pressures ($3.5 - 5.0\text{ MPa}$) increases microscopic contact area, driving $R_{contact}$ down to optimal baseline levels ($2.0 - 8.0\text{ m}\Omega\cdot\text{cm}^2$ for coated BPPs).
